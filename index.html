About

I'm a recent 12th-grade graduate: a math and physics person who ended up writing a lot of code. Most of what I build starts from a first-principles question (how a fluid actually behaves, why an aircraft failed the way it did) and turns into software once I need to answer it at scale.

I'm based in Palakkad, Kerala, India, and split my time between independent research, software engineering, and CFD tooling. I'd rather derive something from scratch than take a black box's word for it.

All of it (the solver work, the fine-tuning, the analysis) was built on a single underpowered laptop. No workstation, no cluster. If the hardware's a bottleneck, that's a problem to work around, not a reason to wait.

CakeCFD

A full CFD workflow from CAD import through meshing, solving, and analysis. TENO convection schemes shipped as a wmake library, STEP/BREP geometry import via OpenCASCADE, a guided blockMesh/snappyHexMesh pipeline, and a native Qt5 + VTK viewport with live residual plots and streamline tracing. Also wires up direct Johns Hopkins Turbulence Database access for DNS inflow data. Two public repos, both GPLv3: the Cake Studio desktop app and the cakecfd_ai control layer.

Learning Experiences

// rejected, Journal of Fluid Mechanics

Intermolecular Navier–Stokes Extension

A proposed thermodynamically-consistent extension to the Navier–Stokes equations for hypersonic continuum flow, adding an intermolecular pressure term via a van der Waals attractive contribution with Redlich–Kwong temperature scaling.

Submitted to the Journal of Fluid Mechanics and rejected after peer review.

What I learned from it
  • The difference between a real scientific claim and an interesting one
  • Scoping a research question against existing literature instead of in isolation
  • Moderating claims to match what the evidence actually supports
  • Which validation methods are accepted in the field, and which aren't
  • What counts as an acceptable error margin for a claim like this
  • How to properly structure a derivation for a reviewer, not just for myself
  • A detailed review report, with the JFM deputy editor, Prof. Meneveau, personally investing time to guide the feedback
  • The referees went beyond the rejection itself, giving detailed guidance for how to approach future submissions
